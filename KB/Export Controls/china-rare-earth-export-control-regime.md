# China Rare Earth Export Control Regime — Reference

**Status as of: 30 July 2026.**
**⚠ This regime changes on 11 November 2026.** Six suspended measures may resume that day. Do not present the position below as current after that date without re-checking.

**Verified against primary Chinese-language source text, 30 July 2026.**

---

## The single most important fact

**China's rare-earth export controls were not suspended.** They are widely reported as having been paused in November 2025. That reporting is wrong, or at least badly incomplete.

The November 2025 stand-down suspended **six** measures. It did **not** touch **Announcement 18 (2025)** — the measure that actually governs terbium and dysprosium. That has required export licences continuously since **4 April 2025** and has since been expanded.

If asked whether China's rare-earth controls are suspended, the correct answer is: **partially — the extraterritorial and technology layers are suspended, the material licensing layer is live.**

## Live now

### Announcement 18 (2025) — effective 4 April 2025

Never suspended.

**Elements covered:** samarium (钐), gadolinium (钆), **terbium (铽)**, **dysprosium (镝)**, lutetium (镥), scandium (钪), yttrium (钇) — as metals, oxides, alloys and compounds.

**Magnet materials covered — this scope is narrow and matters:**

- Samarium-cobalt
- **Terbium-containing NdFeB**
- **Dysprosium-containing NdFeB**

**Not covered: general NdFeB without Tb or Dy.** The control is grade-specific, not category-wide.

### Explicitly exempt — MOFCOM clarifications, September 2025

- **Motor components** — rotors, stator assemblies, sensor components
- **Assembled products** — magnets embedded in cores or steel plates; magnets integrated with shafts, bearings, bushings, fans, gears, balancing plates, encoders
- **Consumer products** — magnetic toys, phone cases, chargers, tablet stands, electromagnetic clamps, machine fixtures
- **Catalytic materials** — catalyst powders and downstream catalytic functional materials
- **Luminescent materials** — phosphors and downstream rare earth luminescent materials

**Consequence for exposure analysis: the perimeter attaches to whoever imports the controlled material, not whoever ships the finished product.** A motor maker importing Dy-grade NdFeB is inside it. A firm exporting a finished robot containing those magnets is outside it.

14 EU entities added to the **Export Control Watchlist** (出口管制管控名单 — note: *not* the Unreliable Entity List, 不可靠实体清单). Chinese exporters may not supply them with dual-use items, including rare earths and permanent magnets, without special authorisation.

### The layered structure

The regime is designed across five layers, and only the bottom one is currently live:

| Layer | Instrument | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Material | **No. 18** | **LIVE** |
| Equipment — means of production | No. 56 | Suspended |
| Technology — know-how | No. 62 | Suspended |
| Finished goods — extraterritorial | No. 61 | Suspended |
| Consumable tooling | No. 55 | Suspended |

**No. 61 is what removes the finished-goods exemption.** A ≥0.1% de minimis rule on foreign-made products captures assembled goods wherever they are made — the exact carve-out that leaves finished robots outside the perimeter today.

**Two measures target the upgrade path rather than current production.** No. 56 controls grain boundary diffusion equipment — the process the industry relies on for high-coercivity magnets. No. 58's 300 Wh/kg threshold sits just above mainstream cells (250–300) and squarely across the next generation (semi-solid 350–400, solid-state 450+). Neither binds much today; both bind on where the industry is going.

## Linked US measure

The **BIS Affiliates Rule** was suspended on 10 November 2025, runs through **9 November 2026**, and **reimposes automatically on 10 November 2026** unless BIS acts.

The two suspensions were explicitly traded for one another — BIS suspended in exchange for China suspending its 9 October 2025 measures. **Neither is a timer; both are positions in a live bargain.**

**The US measure reimposes one day before China's package can resume.** That one-day gap makes 10 November an observable signal for 11 November.

## Why Dy and Tb specifically

Standard NdFeB loses roughly **0.65% of its coercivity per °C**, which is untenable in a servo motor under load. Adding dysprosium raises the working ceiling from around **80°C to over 220°C**.

The industry method is **grain boundary diffusion (GBD)** — diffusing Dy or Tb along the grain boundaries of a finished magnet rather than alloying it throughout. GBD uses **70–80% less heavy rare earth** and preserves remanence. Most effective on magnets under 15mm, which covers most motor applications.

This is why high-performance servo grades — including those used by Japanese industrial motor makers — sit inside the control perimeter while commodity magnets do not.

## Supply structure

**Materials:** China holds roughly 90% of global separation and refining capacity and over 90% of magnet manufacturing. Named magnet makers: JL Mag, Ningbo Yunsheng, Zhenghai, plus Shin-Etsu, Proterial and TDK outside China.

**Production equipment — not Chinese-dominated.** **ULVAC** (Japan) supplies vacuum sintering furnaces, strip casters, hydrogen decrepitation units and jet mills to leading global magnet makers. In May 2026 it established new Japan-based production for rare-earth magnet vacuum melting furnaces explicitly to diversify beyond China, with orders expected to roughly triple year on year on European and North American demand. **Bunting** (UK) runs GBD in Europe. **MP Materials** (US) has its own GBD process and a campus at Northlake, Texas.

**Implication:** the material constraint is hard, but the substitution route is open — on a two-to-three year build cycle. Treating rare-earth dependency as a permanent structural feature over-extrapolates.

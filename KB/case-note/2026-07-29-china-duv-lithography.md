# CASE NOTE #3 — 2026-07-29
- Event: 2026-07-27, The Information reported Shanghai Aishengna (state-backed, absorbed the Yuliangsheng and SMEE teams; Yuliangsheng linked to Huawei/SiCarrier) began limited production of domestic immersion DUV lithography. ~5 units ship 2026 to SMIC, Hua Hong and CXMT; ~20 planned for 2027. Spec'd for 28nm single-exposure and 7nm (possibly 5nm) via multi-patterning; external estimates place it near ASML's NXT:1950i generation. SMIC has been testing a Yuliangsheng tool since Sept 2025. Mostly domestic components, some parts still imported. No throughput or overlay-stability data disclosed.
- Market reaction: ASML -8% intraday Mon 7/27, ~-12% over two sessions to $1,573.46 — lowest since June, testing $1,600 support. Western WFE complex sold off alongside (AMAT, LRCX, KLA). ASML had already guided China to ~20% of 2026 revenue, down from ~33% in 2025; China was 19% of Q1 2026 system sales vs 36% in Q4 2025.
- What everyone misses: This tool does not advance China's node capability by a single nanometer — SMIC already runs 7nm on ASML immersion. What it removes is the *hostage value* of ASML's servicing relationship. Two consequences nobody priced: (1) a full US DUV export ban just got cheap to impose, because the leverage it was preserving is gone — the bipartisan bill already in play becomes the live risk, not Chinese competition; (2) Chinese fabs can now commit capex that fear of a tool cutoff had throttled, pointing at a 2028 mature-node capacity flood. The revenue math on 5 machines is noise; the policy and capex second-order effects are not. Qualification, not shipment, is the real gate — production-grade overlay stability and wafers-per-hour remain undisclosed.
- My read: Fade the ASML panic tactically — order book sold out through 2027 and China was already half-written-off in guidance. But structurally zero China out of ASML's long-run TAM rather than haircut it. Proxies: long domestic WFE — Naura (002371.SZ), AMEC (688012.SS), ACM Research (ACMR) — against ASML and AMAT. **Amended 2026-07-30:** the short leg originally read "the Western complex (ASML, AMAT, LRCX)". LRCX is removed. The thesis here is China TAM and a 2028 mature-node flood; ASML fits (China ~20% of 2026 revenue, lithography, most exposed to a servicing ban) and AMAT fits best (high China share, mature-node heavy). LRCX is etch/deposition and the most NAND-levered of the three, so SK hynix's 2026-07-28 capex raise to ≥₩45tn — including M17 for NAND and PKG & Test 7 for advanced packaging — is a tailwind to it. As originally written this note would have booked a loss on LRCX for a reason it never argued. KLA was never in this proxy list; see Note #6, which narrows this note rather than contradicting it. Cautious on mature-node margin holders into 2027–28: UMC, Hua Hong (1347.HK), TXN.
- Watch: ASML Q3 results ~mid-Oct 2026 — China % of system sales and first 2027 commentary. Whether the 5 units ship *and* qualify at SMIC by Dec 2026. US DUV export ban bill progress — the bill is the MATCH Act (H.R. 8170, House; companion in Senate), introduced Apr 2026 and **already reported out of House Foreign Affairs**. It would impose country-wide DUV prohibitions on China, designate five Chinese semi firms as restricted entities by statute, and give allies 150 days to match before the FDPR expands unilaterally; the servicing ban on installed tools is its most powerful provision. Corrected 2026-07-30: this catalyst is live now, not autumn-pending as originally written. SMIC Q3 capex guidance, early Nov 2026.

## UPDATE — 2026-07-30: the optics column, and where the chokepoint actually sits

- Event: Research into the optical supply chain beneath the Aishengna/Yuliangsheng immersion DUV tool. The projection-optics supplier for Chinese immersion DUV is **Beijing Guowang Optics (国望光学)** — wholly state-owned, incorporated 2018-06-01, ¥3bn registered capital, jointly invested by the Beijing Municipal Government and Chinese Academy of Sciences units, chartered explicitly for immersion lithography exposure optical systems, and the optical-system supplier to SMEE's immersion DUV programme. Yuliangsheng reorganised its architecture around light source, projection optics, dual wafer stages and immersion control from the outset. Aishengna's specific optics vendor is not publicly disclosed. Separately: Zeiss states it does not export EUV technology to China and is actively reducing its own China supply-chain dependence; China placed 14 EU entities, led by Rheinmetall, on its export-control list in July 2026.
- Market reaction: No discrete price event; this is supply-chain diligence on the 07-27 report rather than a new catalyst.
- What everyone misses: The chokepoint is not optical *design or integration* — Guowang has been purpose-built for that for eight years with state capital, and treating the optics column as an unsolved problem is out of date. It is optical **materials**: lithographic-grade fused silica and calcium fluoride blanks of exceptional purity and homogeneity, historically sourced from a very small number of German and Japanese suppliers (Schott, Ohara), plus precision positioning (Physik Instrumente) and laser-source components (Coherent, Trumpf). Tom's Hardware reports some critical parts still come from Japan and that local-supplier delays have held back Aishengna's production — which is the concrete form of the qualification gate this note already flagged. **Correction to the working list:** UP Optotech, Focuslight, MLOptic, Doti Micro and Optowide appear in supply-chain databases as Chinese optics firms but sit in laser optics, optical components and metrology, not the scanner projection column; they should not be read as Aishengna's optics suppliers. The one that carries real signal is Optowide sourcing optical glass from Schott, because it corroborates the materials dependency rather than the integration one.
- My read: This **cuts against this note's own structural conclusion** and I am marking it rather than burying it. "Zero China out of ASML's long-run TAM" assumed the domestic tool ramps. If the binding constraint is German and Japanese optical materials, then allied export control has a second lever that never requires banning ASML at all — constrain the sub-tier instead — and the 5-unit-2026 to ~20-unit-2027 ramp is materially more fragile than this note assumed. That preserves ASML's China position longer than structural zeroing implies, so the structural leg should be treated as a haircut with a policy-dependent tail rather than a zero, pending evidence the 2027 ramp holds. What it *strengthens* is this note's central claim that policy, not Chinese competition, is the live variable — and it gives the MATCH Act's 150-day allied-matching clock real teeth, since the constraint that matters is precisely the allied one. The tactical fade is unchanged.
- Watch: Any German export-control decision covering semiconductor-related optics or optical glass. Whether Aishengna's ~20-unit 2027 plan slips — the cleanest read on whether the sub-tier gate binds. Guowang Optics capacity and yield disclosure. Any Schott or Ohara commentary on lithographic-grade blank shipments to China. Whether the MATCH Act's enacted text reaches sub-tier components rather than only finished systems.

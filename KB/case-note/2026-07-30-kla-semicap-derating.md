# CASE NOTE #6 — 2026-07-30
- Event: KLA reported FQ4 FY26 after the close Tue 2026-07-28 — record quarterly revenue $3.66bn (+15% YoY), GAAP net income $1.36bn, GAAP diluted EPS $1.04; FY26 revenue $13.58bn, GAAP EPS $3.66. FQ1'27 guided to $3.8–4.2bn revenue (midpoint above ~$3.9bn consensus) and $1.06–1.26 EPS, with backlog reaching ~$12.5bn at FY26 close and demand described as broadening across foundry logic, DRAM, NAND and advanced packaging rather than resting on a single leading-edge customer. Management simultaneously flagged conservative WFE spending assumptions and tier-one customer installation deferrals compressing near-term revenue visibility. One day earlier, Tue 2026-07-28, SK hynix reported Q2: revenue ₩79.3tn (+257% YoY), operating profit ₩60.5tn, a record 76% operating margin — and raised FY26 capex ~50% to at least ₩45tn (~$31bn), accelerating M15X and Yongin Fab One and adding PKG & Test 7 (advanced packaging) and M17 (NAND).
- Market reaction: KLAC $203.36 → $190.80 (Tue 07-28, −6.18%) → **$170.19 (Wed 07-29, −10.80%)**. Base note: KLA executed a 10-for-1 forward split and has traded split-adjusted since Fri 2026-06-12, so no price before that date is comparable. SK hynix's record print was received as a disappointment against elevated expectations, with the capex line identified as the culprit. Sector-wide: SOX −19% month-to-date, worst month since 2008, every member below its 50-day moving average; Nasdaq 100 in correction after a hawkish Fed hold (three dissents for a hike, 10-year to 4.67%); TAIEX −3.76% on 07-29. Nvidia's ~$750bn of pending AI infrastructure deals pushed its 5-year CDS to a record 82bp.
- What everyone misses: The same number is being read as bearish twice. SK hynix raising capex 50% is punished at the spender as cash burn, then punished again at the supplier whose revenue line it literally is — KLA's process control and packaging inspection sit directly beneath PKG & Test 7 and M17. Both readings cannot be right. What changed in July is not demand but the regime for interpreting capex: the AI trade flipped from "capex equals growth" to "capex equals debt," and in that regime an order book is treated as a liability rather than an asset. The tell is that the de-rating is hitting hardest where the China risk has already been removed — KLA's China share went 43% → 33% → ~26–31% across two years, so the "China floods the market" narrative that triggered this week is being applied to a revenue base that has already left. Meanwhile the one genuinely soft disclosure, tier-one installation deferrals, is a revenue-recognition timing item on tools already ordered rather than an order cancellation — and it is the part nobody is actually trading.
- My read: The 10.8% single-session repricing overshoots on the large Western semicap name with the least remaining China exposure and the most direct leverage to the memory capex cycle that just accelerated.

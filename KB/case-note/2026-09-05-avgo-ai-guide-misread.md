# CASE NOTE #13 — 2026-09-05
- Event: Broadcom reported Q3 FY26 after the close Wed 2026-09-02 — revenue $29.6bn (+86% YoY, beat the ~$29.25–29.36bn consensus), adjusted EPS $3.32 (beat ~$3.21–3.24 est), operating income $20.1bn (+92% YoY, 68% margin), free cash flow $13.7bn (46% of revenue). AI semiconductor revenue was the headline: $16.7bn (+221% YoY, +54% QoQ), now 56% of total revenue, on six disclosed XPU customers (Google, Meta, OpenAI, Anthropic plus two unnamed hyperscalers). Guidance: Q4 AI semi revenue raised to $21.7bn (+236% YoY), FY26 AI revenue to $58bn — and management gave an unprecedented two-year forward marker on the call: ~$115bn FY27, ~$230bn FY28. Against that, total Q4 revenue was guided to ~$34.8bn, a hair under the ~$35.0bn Street estimate, driven by non-AI semiconductors (~$4.3bn, still small) and infrastructure software/VMware "stabilizing" at ~$8.7bn after Q3's $8.8bn (+29% YoY) — the acquisition-driven software growth spurt cooling.
- Market reaction: AVGO whipsawed the same evening — fell ~3% immediately on the Q4 revenue guide, then popped ~2% when the FY27/28 AI figures came out on the call, settling around -0.8% after hours (~$364). It kept falling into the next session: **$357.16 (Thu 2026-09-03 NASDAQ close, verified)**, down 2.74% from the $367.24 pre-earnings close. Fri 2026-09-04's close could not be confirmed by search — pre-market only showed +1.16% to $361.30; flagging rather than guessing.
- What everyone misses: The number that moved the stock — the $34.8bn vs $35.0bn total-revenue guide — is a 0.7% miss sourced entirely from Broadcom's smallest and slowest segments: non-AI semis (~15% of revenue) and VMware software going from 29% growth to flat. The number that actually matters to this stock's thesis — AI semiconductor revenue — was raised on every horizon: this quarter beat by a mile, next quarter's guide raised to $21.7bn, and management extended the visible backlog out two more years to $115bn and $230bn, implying AI semi revenue roughly doubles in FY27 and doubles again in FY28. That long-range figure is a bigger multi-year AI capex commitment than Nvidia's own forward disclosures, and it got a "beat and fall" headline instead of being the story. Same misread pattern as Note #6 on KLAC: the market treats a rounding-error miss on an unrelated business line as the news while the actual AI capex signal gets stronger underneath it.
- My read: The two-session, ~3.5% pullback is a proximate-metric overreaction on the stock most directly levered to hyperscaler custom-silicon spend — the FY27/28 guide, once it's actually digested rather than talked over on a call, is a bigger structural data point than the Q4 total-revenue guide currently driving the tape. Long **AVGO @ $357.16** — the Thu 2026-09-03 NASDAQ close, first full session after the print. Connects to Note #6 (KLAC): same regime-misread pattern, different supplier — worth grading side by side. Also touches Note #10's Nvidia H200/China thesis without contradicting it: Broadcom's custom-ASIC book (Google, Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta) is the structural alternative to Nvidia GPU dependency that the H200 fade doesn't address — a different leg of the same AI-compute reallocation, not a competing call.
- Watch: FY27 AI revenue guide (~$115bn) starting to show up in quarterly cadence — first checkpoint is Broadcom's FQ1'27 print (expected ~Dec 2026/early 2027, exact date TBD). Whether VMware "stabilizing" at $8.7bn holds or keeps decelerating. Whether the six XPU customers add a seventh (industry chatter has named Apple as a candidate) or any existing one slips a deployment date — OpenAI's own ASIC already slipped from Q2 to Q3 2026 once. Falsification: AVGO trading back below the pre-earnings $367 level within 30 days (by 2026-10-03) on no new negative AI data would say the market's proximate-metric read was right and this note's is wrong.

# CASE NOTE #12 — 2026-08-28
- Event: Lynas FY26 results, 26 Aug 2026. Revenue $977.9M (+76% YoY), NPAT $222.4M (up from $8.0M FY25), EBITDA +282% to A$386.0M, record avg selling price A$80.7/kg REO. Missed Visible Alpha consensus NPAT estimate (A$242.5M) — hence "miss" headlines. Management said the current cost base "is not a good guide for the future" (temporary/ramp-up costs). Separately: Malaysia heavy-rare-earth (HRE) plant capex escalated 63%, A$180M → A$294M, blamed explicitly on non-Chinese equipment sourcing and geopolitical cost inflation.
- Market reaction: Shares fell on results day despite the record NPAT/revenue beat YoY. LYC.AX @ A$16.21 (Wed 08-26 ASX close, verified).
- What everyone misses: "Lynas misses profit forecasts" is a miss against a Street estimate, not against last year — NPAT is up ~28x YoY. The real story is which cost line is temporary and which isn't. Opex is management-flagged as ramp noise. The 63% HRE capex blowout is not — it's a structural "de-China" premium on building rare-earth capacity outside China, showing up in capex rather than opex.
- My read: Pricing power is real (record $80.7/kg), but it's being taxed by a recurring, structural premium for non-Chinese HRE capacity — not a one-off. That premium likely shows up again at MP Materials and any other ex-China buildout; it's a slow bleed on the economics, not a crack in the moat. Long LYC.AX as the cleanest listed proxy for ex-China HRE capacity — the capex overrun is the cost of the moat, not evidence against it. Relates to Note #11 (MP Materials), which already flagged Lynas as the cleaner proxy for the ex-China rare-earth read — this note is the follow-through on that flag, not a contradiction of it.
- Watch: Next Lynas quarterly production/cost report (expected ~late Oct 2026) — does the "temporary" opex actually fall out of the cost base, and does the A$294M HRE budget hold or escalate again. Check-by: 2026-10-31.

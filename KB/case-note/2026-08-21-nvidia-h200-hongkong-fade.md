# CASE NOTE #10 — 2026-08-21
- Event: FT reported 2026-08-18 that ByteDance and Tencent each took delivery of ~10,000 Nvidia H200 GPUs — first real shipments into China since the Dec 2025 export approval. Under the Jan 2026 US licensing framework, each of ~10 cleared buyers (Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance, JD.com, others) can purchase up to 75,000 units; actual deliveries are ~13% of that cap. Beijing's NDRC is directing firms to route the chips through Hong Kong — outside mainland customs, and reportedly short of the power and rack capacity to run them at scale — and now reviews every future H200 purchase case by case.
- Market reaction: NVDA closed $217.56 Wed 2026-08-19 (NASDAQ), -0.99% same day — no distinct pop on the "China reopening" headline; it moved with the broader bond-yield-driven chip selloff instead.
- What everyone misses: the headline reads "Nvidia's back in China." The mechanics say Beijing decided how much control to keep — a trickle at 1/8th the legal ceiling, parked in a jurisdiction that can't power it yet, under a veto China holds shipment-by-shipment. That's not a reopening, it's a leash.
- My read: fade any NVDA rally priced specifically off "China demand reopening" — the real constraint isn't Washington's cap anymore, it's Beijing's willingness to let volume climb past ~13%. NVDA @ $217.56 (Wed 2026-08-19 close) is the only clean listed proxy.
- Watch: whether monthly China H200 deliveries climb past ~13% of the 75,000-unit cap or plateau there; whether Hong Kong actually stands up power/rack capacity for chips already parked; next FT/Reuters follow-up on mainland vs. HK end-use.

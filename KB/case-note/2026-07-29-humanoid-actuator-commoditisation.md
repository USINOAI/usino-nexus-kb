- What everyone misses: Harmonic's humanoid revenue is *prototype* orders — R&D-phase volume from customers who are simultaneously building the capability to never place a production order. Actuators are ~56% of Optimus BOM, which makes them the single largest cost-down target, not a defensible toll booth: Tesla's stated $20k price point requires a 5–10x cost reduction, and there is no arithmetic that gets there while paying Japanese strain-wave margins. Tesla builds its own custom motors and gearboxes explicitly because no global humanoid actuator supply base exists. Unitree went further and designed harmonic reducers out of the architecture entirely, substituting self-developed planetary units with optimised tooth profiles, and landed the R1 at $4,160. So the actuator supplier rally is being fed by precisely the orders that vanish at scale — every serious volume player is a temporary customer, and the better a supplier's prototype pipeline looks, the more accurately it is forecasting its own disintermediation.
- My read: This contradicts my own Note #4 and I am marking it against that note deliberately. "Own the actuator layer, not the OEM brand" treated actuators as a toll booth; the correct frame is that the largest BOM line is the most contested line, therefore the least defensible. The tell is Unitree — first profitable year built entirely on full-stack in-house actuators, and profit still halved YoY on competition. Vertical integration wins the cost war and then commoditises the thing it integrated; the winner of the actuator layer captures no rent from it. Call: fade 6324.T into humanoid-driven rallies — 6324.T @ ¥5,780 is the entry. Schaeffler survives the argument but for the opposite reason to Note #4: its exposure is deployment, equity and customer relationship (Humanoid UK, own-plant fleet), which is an option on not being disintermediated, not a component-supply annuity.
